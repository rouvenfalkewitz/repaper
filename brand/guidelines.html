RePaper

Reusable paper. Printed the way you already print.

The band above is an ordered dither from ink to paper — the brand's texture. Real 1-bit dithering, the same thing our renderer does to every page. Use it as the one decorative motif; never gradients.

01

Identity & logo

RePaper is a printer that prints onto reusable e-paper. The brand should feel like the product: matte, high-contrast, plain and confident — with one warm, playful note.

Name

RePaper, always one word, capital R and capital P. In code, domains and handles: repaper. Never "Re-Paper", "rePaper" or "RE PAPER". The "Re" is the whole idea — reusable, reprint, rethink — so the wordmark sets it in Re Green.

Wordmark

The wordmark is typographic: Bricolage Grotesque Bold, tracking −3%, "Re" in Re Green 500, "Paper" in Ink. On dark or coloured grounds use the single-colour version. This is usable today, before a logo mark exists.

Logo mark

The mark is being generated with an AI tool from the brief in brand/logo-prompt.md. Direction: a sheet with a folded corner where the fold becomes a return arrow — "the page comes back". Requirements the result must pass, whatever it looks like:

Logo mark — pending
Reserve a square slot. The mark must read at 16 px, in pure 1-bit black, embossed on plastic, and as an LED diffuser silhouette.
  • Mark + wordmark side by side, mark cap-height aligned to the "R"
  • Clear space around the lockup = the height of the "e"
  • Minimum size: mark 16 px, wordmark 80 px wide
  • Mono versions in Ink or Paper only
  • No gradients, no shadows, no 3D, no gloss
  • No outline or "hollow" versions
  • Don't recolour "Re" in anything but Re Green or the mono ink
  • Don't put the wordmark on photos without a solid panel behind it

Dark mode

Ground becomes Ink #131614, surfaces #1C201D, borders #2E3430, text Paper. Accent fills stay Re Green 500 with Ink text; accent text becomes #2FCB9F. Dark mode is the device's own look — the Dock's web UI defaults to it.

On-sheet palette

Content rendered to an e-paper sheet uses the panel's physical colours, not brand tokens: black #000000, white #FFFFFF, red #C8102E (BWR), yellow #F2C400 (BWRY). Renders are 1-bit per plane; everything in between is dithered.

On the sheet

Text rendered onto e-paper is 1-bit: no anti-aliasing, no thin strokes. Use Figtree 500 or 600, never lighter. Minimum sizes at the usual ~130 ppi of a 4.2" panel: 12 px body, 9 px mono. Prefer a strong hierarchy (one big line, few small ones) over many medium lines; from a metre away only the headline survives.

Tokens --s-1 … --s-9 (4 → 96). Inside a component: 8–16. Between components: 16–24. Between sections: 48–96.

Radius

Sheets and previews of sheets are nearly square-cornered (4 px) — e-paper panels are. Controls 6 px, cards 12 px. Nothing else.

Surfaces & elevation

Cards are Sheet on Paper with a 1 px Line border. No drop shadows on resting elements. Only floating things — toasts, menus, dialogs — get a single soft shadow (0 8px 24px rgba(0,0,0,.18)). Dividers are 1 px Line, full width.

Page grid

Content max-width 1180 px; reading text max 64 characters. Two-column layouts split 220 / rest (navigation / content) or 1 / 1. On mobile everything stacks with 16 px side padding. Left-aligned by default; centre only single short statements.

Motion

Motion is functional and short. The one signature move is borrowed from the hardware: the e-ink refresh flash.

TokenValueUse
--dur-fast150 msHover, press, toggles
--dur-base220 msReveal, slide, panel open
--dur-slow400 msPage transitions, sheet preview updates
--ease-outcubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)Everything that enters
eink-refresh1.6 s, 4 steps of invertWhen a sheet preview gets new content; as a loading state

The refresh flash is a stepped animation (steps(1)) — never eased, never faded. It's what a real panel does. Use it once per event, never looping longer than three seconds. LED patterns are stepped too. Respect prefers-reduced-motion: replace the flash with an instant swap and the LED blink pill with a static dot.

08

Voice & language

We sell "it's just a printer". The words should never make it sound like more than that.

Principles

  • Plain. "Hold a sheet to print." Short verbs, present tense.
  • Confident. Say what happened: "Printed." Not "Successfully completed the print operation".
  • Warm. A little delight is allowed at the moment of magic — the print landing on the sheet.
  • Green with numbers. "2,140 sheets of paper saved this month." Never "eco-friendly".
  • No IoT / tech vocabulary in customer-facing copy: gateway, sync, provision, firmware, tag, ESL, MQTT.
  • No exclamation marks in system messages.
  • No apologising in error messages — say what went wrong and what to do.
  • No "smart" as an adjective. The point is that nothing is smart; it's a printer.
