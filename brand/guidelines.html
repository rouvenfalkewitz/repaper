Brand guide · v1.0

RePaper

Reusable paper. Printed the way you already print.

This is the brand at full volume: Carbon ground, one flat luminous colour, a wide caps wordmark, and a strip of real 1-bit dither — the same thing our renderer does to every page. Booths, merch, the website hero and the Dock itself all look like this. Product UI turns the volume down (see Color).

01 · Identity

Identity & logo

RePaper is a printer that prints onto reusable e-paper. The brand is built like a modern hardware start-up: one luminous colour on carbon, confident type, soft shapes, no noise.

Name

RePaper — one word, capital R and P in prose. The wordmark is set in caps. repaper in code, domains and handles. Never "Re-Paper", "rePaper", "RE PAPER".

Wordmark

Archivo Extra-Bold, width 125 %, caps, tracking −3 %. "RE" in Re Green, "PAPER" in Paper (on dark) or Ink (on light). The wordmark and the mark never glow — flat colour everywhere, on screen and in print. Glow is reserved for interactive elements (the primary button) and for real light (the Dock's ring, LED strips, edge-lit signs), which keeps the logo one file, one colour, one rule.

Logo mark

Generated from the brief in brand/logo-prompt.md. Direction: a sheet whose folded corner becomes a return arrow — drawn with the same blocky, even-stroke geometry as the wordmark's letters, so the mark and the "R" feel cut from the same material. Must pass, whatever it ends up looking like:

LOGO MARK — PENDING
Square slot. Reads at 16 px · in pure 1-bit black · embossed on plastic · as the glowing silhouette behind the Dock's LED diffuser.
  • Mark left of the wordmark, mark height = cap height of "R"
  • Clear space around the lockup = the height of the "E"
  • Minimum: mark 16 px, wordmark 90 px wide
  • Mono versions in Carbon, Paper or Re Green only
  • No gradients inside the logo, no 3D, no outline-only versions
  • No glow, shadow or halo on the wordmark or mark — anywhere
  • Never lower-case or condensed wordmark
  • Don't put the wordmark on photos without a carbon panel behind it

  • Primary button = Re Green fill + Carbon/Ink text, on both grounds
  • Glow (box-shadow) only on screens showing carbon, only on the primary button and on LED renders — never on type or the logo
  • In print: Re Green is a flat spot colour on black — the "printed glow" is a 1-bit dither fade
  • One accent per screen; Carbon, Mist and Paper do the rest
  • Charts: Re 200/400/700 + Mist
  • No gradients, anywhere — if something needs a transition, dither it
  • No glow in print, ever: no halos, no soft edges, no gradients on fabric or paper
  • No Re Glow text on light grounds (fails contrast badly)
  • No second accent, no orange, no purple
  • Don't use Re Green to mean "success" and as decoration on the same screen

On-sheet palette

Content rendered to e-paper uses the panel's physical colours: black #000000, white #FFFFFF, red #C8102E (BWR), yellow #F2C400 (BWRY). 1-bit per plane; everything else is dithered.

On the sheet

1-bit rendering: no anti-aliasing, no thin strokes. Archivo 700 for the headline (it fits long location codes), Figtree 500/600 for body, JetBrains Mono for codes. Minimum 12 px body / 9 px mono on a 4.2" panel. One big line, few small ones — from a metre away only the headline survives.

Layout & spacing

Sheets stay nearly square (4 px) because e-paper panels are. Controls 8, cards 16, hardware silhouettes 28, tags are pills. Buttons are 8 px — not pills — so they read as buttons next to pill tags.

Surfaces

On Carbon: surfaces step up one Carbon tone per level (Carbon → 2 → 3) with a 1 px Carbon-4 border. Floating elements get 0 12px 32px rgba(0,0,0,.5). On Paper: Sheet cards with a Line border, no resting shadow. Dividers 1 px, full width. Content max 1180 px, reading text 64 characters, left-aligned; centre only single short statements and the booth wall.

One RGB light ring behind a frosted diffuser. Green means go, red means stop, blue means "talking to you about setup", amber means "your call". Patterns are stepped, like the pills.

07 · Merch & booth

Merch & booth

Off-screen the brand is at full volume, always: Carbon everything, Re Green as light. Glow is light, never ink. If something is a light source, it glows. If it's printed or stitched, it's flat, solid Re Green on black — and where it wants to feel luminous, it uses a dither fade instead.

The printed "glow"A 1-bit dither fade from Re Green into black — two flat colours, two screens, prints on anything. This is how print says luminous without a gradient.
The DockMatte carbon, 28 px-radius silhouette, frosted light ring. Real light, so it really glows. On a booth, five of them breathing in sync is the whole demo.
  • Black + Re Green + white type. Nothing else, ever, on merch.
  • Print Re Green as a flat spot colour on black. Screen inks can't reach #1EE3A5 exactly — pick one Pantone from a physical swatch book (neon-mint range, around 3385 C / 7479 C, or a fluorescent) and that becomes "Re Green, printed" everywhere
  • Fluorescent ink or thread on black gets closest to the on-screen colour
  • Real light where possible: LED strips, edge-lit acrylic logos, the Dock's ring
  • Dither fade as the printed "glow"; dither strip as texture on walls, lanyards, bags
  • No white merch — the brand doesn't exist on white
  • No printed glow, halo, drop-shadow or gradient — not on fabric, paper, vinyl or walls
  • No metallic or foil greens; flat only
  • No taglines on merch; the wordmark carries it
  • No "eco" imagery — leaves, earths, recycling arrows
08 · Motion

Motion

Short, functional, stepped where the hardware is stepped. Two signatures: the e-ink refresh flash, and the light-ring breathe.

TokenValueUse
--dur-fast150 msHover, press, toggles

--ease-outcubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)Everything that enters

The refresh flash is steps(1) — never eased. The breathe is the only slow, eased loop allowed. Respect prefers-reduced-motion: instant swaps, static dots, no breathe.

09 · Voice

Voice & language

We sell "it's just a printer". Plain and confident in sentences; short, caps and operational in states.

  • Plain. "Hold a sheet to print." Short verbs, present tense.
  • Confident. "Printed." Not "Successfully completed".
  • Operational in states. Pills and LED labels are caps, two words max.
  • Green with numbers. "2,140 sheets of paper saved this month."
  • No IoT vocabulary towards customers: gateway, sync, provision, firmware, tag, ESL.
  • No exclamation marks, no emoji in the product.
  • No apologising in errors — say what to do.
  • No "smart", no "eco". It's a printer.
