Notes

  • A · Dissolve — strongest idea and most "ours" (it is literally the brand's dither motif). Weakness: not stencil-safe (loose pixels) and it's the least readable at 16 px; as a favicon it becomes "a square with a rough edge", which is acceptable but not great. Silhouette (full square) is fine for the diffuser.
  • B · Pixel-leg R — most memorable and most confident next to the wordmark; reads at every size; stencil-safe; silhouette works. Weakness: it's a letter R, so the lockup shows an R next to "REPAPER" — common practice (many brands do it), but it means the mark leans on the name. Also the most likely to have look-alikes; needs a reverse-image check.
  • C · The pixel that stepped out — simplest and most charming; reads at 16 px; silhouette works. Weakness: the loose pixel is a floating island (not stencil-safe unless it's joined by a bridge), and the idea is quieter — it may need the wordmark to make sense.
  • What was dropped: a single-line rounded spiral (read as a lowercase "a"), a "ghost of the previous print" (read as a folder), a half-solid/half-outline flip (read as a toggle).
  • If one of these is close, say what to push — heavier, rounder, more pixels, fewer — and it gets a refinement round. If none, they at least show ChatGPT what "abstract but on-brand" means; you can paste a candidate into the chat as a reference.
