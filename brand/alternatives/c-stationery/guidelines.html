Identity

Identity & logo

RePaper as a thing you'd keep on your desk. The brand borrows from good stationery: a soft serif, fountain-pen ink, real paper edges, and a little wit.

Name

RePaper — one word, capital R and P. The wordmark is Fraunces Bold with the "wonky" axis on, so the letters have a hand-cut feel; "Re" in Ink Blue, "Paper" in Umber. repaper in code.

Logo mark

Direction for the mark: a folded corner — just the dog-ear of a page, drawn as one soft shape, with the fold's inner edge forming a small "r". Quiet, warm, instantly "paper". Also works as a physical detail: the top-right corner of every Sheet case and of the Dock is the same fold.

Logo mark — pending
Square slot. Soft corners; must still read in 1-bit.
  • Mark sits left of the wordmark, x-height aligned
  • Clear space = the width of the "e"
  • Mono in Umber, Cotton or Ink Blue
  • Let it be small; it doesn't need to shout
  • No gradients, no paper texture behind the logo
  • No italic wordmark
  • Don't add a tagline to the lockup
  • Don't use Stamp Red in the logo, ever

Blue is the only colour that means "RePaper". Red, moss and honey mean their state and nothing else. Never tint the ground; the paper stays cotton.

Dark mode

A warm dark: ground #1C1916, surfaces #272320, text Cotton, accent #8FA7FF. Buttons stay Ink Blue 500 with white text. Paper shadows become a little deeper rather than disappearing — cards still stack.

On the sheet

Sheets look like typed or hand-set paper: Fraunces 600 for the headline, Courier Prime for lists and detail (typewriter faces dither beautifully at 1-bit). Minimum 12 px for Courier on a 4.2" panel. Generous margins — a sheet should never look crammed.

Voice

Voice

Like a good note left on the kitchen table: friendly, specific, a little dry. We're allowed to be charming here — just never cute.

  • Talk to one person: "your sheet", "your phone".
  • Small, true details: "printed 3× this week".
  • Full sentences with full stops. Contractions are fine.
  • Humour in the empty states, never in the errors.
  • No exclamation marks, no emoji in the product.
  • No tech vocabulary: gateway, sync, provision, firmware, tag.
  • No "eco", "green", "smart".
  • No apologising — say what to do instead.
